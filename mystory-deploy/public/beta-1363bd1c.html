What it is

Most services in this space send your mother weekly emails and hope she writes back. Most people don't. This works the other way around: you interview her.

A guide named Grace spends about twenty minutes learning who you're interviewing and what matters to your family. Then she builds you a plan for five conversations and prepares you for each one — what to ask, what to listen for, what to leave alone. You have the conversation, record it or type up notes, and each one becomes a chapter. At the end it's a hardcover book.

What I'm asking of you

  • Interview someone real. A parent, a grandparent, an aunt, a neighbour, someone from your church. Someone whose stories you'd actually want written down.
  • Give it real time. Twenty minutes of setup, then five conversations. Most run 45 to 90 minutes. Expect six to ten hours spread across a month or two — this isn't something you finish in an evening.
  • Tell me the truth about it. Where it was confusing, where Grace got it wrong, where you nearly gave up. The complaints are worth more to me than the compliments.
  • Tell me if you stop. If you get to conversation two and stall, that's not a failure — it's the single most useful thing I could learn. Just tell me where and why.

What you get

  • The whole thing free, including a printed hardcover copy.
  • Extra copies at cost if other family members want one.
  • Your stories stay yours. They're not used to train anything, not shared, not published. If you want everything deleted afterwards, say the word and it's gone.

Things you should know before you say yes.

This is early software. You will hit bugs. Something will break at an inconvenient moment and I'll be fixing it while you wait.

The writing is done by AI from your recording or transcript. It's good, but it's not a person, and you'll want to read every chapter before it goes in the book. You can edit anything.

Please ask the person you're interviewing whether they're comfortable being recorded, and mean it. Some of what comes up in these conversations is private, and they should get to decide what's in the book.
